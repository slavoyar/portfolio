# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters and hiring managers evaluating Iaroslav Zaprudskii for full-stack roles. They arrive with limited time — often between meetings or while screening a stack of candidates — and need a fast read on technical fit, experience depth, and whether reaching out is worth it.

## Product Purpose

A one-page personal portfolio that establishes credibility as a full-stack developer and makes contact effortless. Success is a recruiter understanding who Iaroslav is, what he has built, and feeling confident enough to email, message on Telegram, or connect on LinkedIn.

The site should demonstrate frontend craft in its own execution: if the portfolio looks like a generic template, it undermines the message.

## Brand Personality

Precise, technical, understated.

Voice is direct and competent — no hype, no buzzword padding. The site should feel like it was built by someone who cares about structure, clarity, and detail. Confidence comes from restraint and quality, not decoration.

## Anti-references

- Generic dark developer portfolios: zinc card grids, blue link accents, pill tag clouds, identical section cards
- SaaS landing page scaffolding: uppercase tracked eyebrows, hero metrics, numbered section markers
- Over-designed surfaces: heavy motion, glassmorphism, gradient text, visual noise that competes with content
- Resume-as-PDF layouts: dense walls of text with no hierarchy or breathing room

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach** — The portfolio itself is proof of frontend competence; accessibility, performance, and craft are non-negotiable.
2. **Respect the recruiter's scan** — Lead with signal (role, experience, projects); make contact obvious without begging for it.
3. **Show, don't tell** — Projects and work history carry more weight than adjective-heavy bio copy.
4. **Understated confidence** — Distinction through precision and hierarchy, not decoration or template patterns.
5. **Bilingual by default** — EN/RU support should feel native, not bolted on; every surface string belongs in locale files.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline. Keyboard navigation with visible focus states, sufficient color contrast, semantic landmarks, and descriptive labels on all interactive elements. Reduced-motion alternatives required if motion is added later.
